# Nürburgring Nordschleife — Researched Facts (history, layout, elevation)

Research date: 1 July 2026. All facts below were gathered and cross-checked from web
sources; nothing is invented. Items that could not be fully verified are flagged.

## 1. Track length (which figure is which)

- **20.832 km** — the full Nordschleife lap in its current (post-1982/83) configuration,
  using the short link/bypass that skips the modern GP circuit. Established when a bypass
  with a small pit lane was added during the 1982–83 rebuild.
  Sources: [Wikipedia — Nürburgring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N%C3%BCrburgring),
  [RacingCircuits.info](https://www.racingcircuits.info/europe/germany/nurburgring.html),
  [oversteer48 — How long is the Nordschleife](https://oversteer48.com/how-long-is-the-nurburgring-nordschleife/)
- **20.6 km (20,600 m)** — the shortened configuration used by industry testers and
  *sport auto* magazine for timed laps ("about 200 m shorter for safety reasons"); the
  figure behind most older manufacturer lap-time claims.
  Sources: [Wikipedia — List of Nordschleife lap times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_N%C3%BCrburgring_Nordschleife_lap_times),
  [oversteer48](https://oversteer48.com/how-long-is-the-nurburgring-nordschleife/)
- **19.1 km** — "Bridge to Gantry" (BTG), the informal tourist-lap timing distance
  (the main straight can't be driven at speed during Touristenfahrten).
  Source: [oversteer48](https://oversteer48.com/how-long-is-the-nurburgring-nordschleife/)
- **Combined GP + Nordschleife layouts:** **25.378 km** — the 24h-race layout used since
  2005 — and **24.358 km** — the shorter combined variant used for the 2004 N24 and by
  VLN/NLS since 2007.
  Sources: [Wikipedia — Nürburgring 24 Hours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N%C3%BCrburgring_24_Hours),
  [Wikipedia — List of Nordschleife lap times (racing)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Nordschleife_lap_times_(racing)),
  [European Speed Club — Nürburgring 24 Hours](https://www.european-speed-club.com/en/post/nuerburgring-24-hours)
- Original (1927–1982) Nordschleife: **22.810 km**.
  Source: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N%C3%BCrburgring)

## 2. Construction and opening

- Construction approved 13 Aug 1925, foundation stone laid 27 Sep 1925; built
  1925–spring 1927 as an **unemployment-relief/employment project for the poor Eifel
  region**, with up to **2,300 workers** employed at once.
  Sources: [nuerburgring.de — 100 years: construction approval](https://nuerburgring.de/news/100-jahre-nuerburgring-13-08-1925-genehmigung-zum-bau-der-nordschleife),
  [nuerburgring.de — foundation stone](https://nuerburgring.de/news/100-jahre-nuerburgring-27-september-1925-vor-100-jahren-wurde-der-grundstein-des-nuerburgrings-gelegt?locale=en),
  [dailysportscar.com](https://www.dailysportscar.com/2025/08/13/work-began-on-building-the-nurburgring-100-years-ago-today.html)
- **Opened 18 June 1927** with the Eifelrennen motorcycle race; the first car race
  followed on **19 June 1927**, won by Rudolf Caracciola.
  Sources: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N%C3%BCrburgring),
  [Hagerty](https://www.hagerty.com/media/automotive-history/nurburgring-first-race-this-day-in-1927/),
  [evo — Birth of an icon](https://www.evo.co.uk/features/features/236701/birth_of_an_icon_1927_the_nrburgring.html)
- Original **Gesamtstrecke** (Nordschleife + Südschleife): **28.265 km**.
  Sources: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N%C3%BCrburgring),
  [Circuits of the Past](https://www.circuitsofthepast.com/nurburgring-circuit/)

## 3. Width, corners, elevation

- **Corners:** officially **73** (33 left, 40 right) in the current configuration; the
  original full track had 174 bends before the 1971 changes.
  Sources: [nuerburgring.de — Nordschleife](https://www.nuerburgring.de/en/fans-info/race-tracks/nordschleife.html),
  [Hyundai Driving Experience Nordschleife Guide](https://eu.drivingexperience.hyundai.com/blogs/news/nordschleife-guide),
  [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N%C3%BCrburgring)
- **Width:** the original track "averaged 8 to 9 metres in width"
  ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N%C3%BCrburgring)). *FLAG: no official
  figure for today's width verified; sources describe it only as "narrow with limited
  run-off".*
- **Elevation:** lowest point **~320 m** at Breidscheid; highest **~617 m** (616.8 m)
  near Hohe Acht; difference **~297–300 m**.
  Sources: [nordschleife-btg.net — Gradient of altitude](https://www.nordschleife-btg.net/nordschleife/gradient-of-altitude/),
  [Big Garage](https://www.biggarage.de/info/nuerburgring.html),
  [climbfinder](https://climbfinder.com/en/climbs/hohe-acht-nurburgring-nordschleife-breidscheid)
- **Gradients:** maximum uphill **17%**, maximum downhill **11%**
  ([nordschleife-btg.net](https://www.nordschleife-btg.net/nordschleife/gradient-of-altitude/),
  [Big Garage](https://www.biggarage.de/info/nuerburgring.html)). One source cites up to
  18% between Karussell and Hohe Acht — 17% is the standard published figure.

## 4. "Green Hell" nickname

Coined by **Jackie Stewart** after his **1968 German Grand Prix** victory, driven in
torrential rain and dense fog with a broken wrist, winning by around **4 minutes** —
F1's biggest winning margin at the 'Ring.
Sources: [Formula1.com — F1's Best Drives #1](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/f1s-best-drives-1-stewart-conquers-the-green-hell.466hYmp1yJAiNY0vZXpx0r),
[Automobilist — Stewart at the Nürburgring](https://automobilist.com/en-us/blogs/stories/sir-jackie-stewart-at-the-nurburgring-through-hell-and-back)

## 5. Key historical moments

- **1970 boycott:** F1 drivers led by Jackie Stewart boycotted the Nürburgring over
  safety; the 1970 German GP moved to Hockenheim. The track was rebuilt for **1971** —
  smoothed, straightened, fitted with Armco and run-off — and hosted the GP until 1976.
  Sources: [Wikipedia — German Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Grand_Prix),
  [Wikipedia — Nürburgring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N%C3%BCrburgring)
- **1976 Niki Lauda crash:** 1 August 1976, lap 2 of the German GP, at the **fast left
  kink between Ex-Mühle and Bergwerk** (unofficially "Lauda-Links"); his Ferrari 312T2
  hit the embankment and burst into flames; fellow drivers (Lunger, Merzario, Edwards,
  Ertl) pulled him out.
  Sources: [Wikipedia — Niki Lauda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niki_Lauda),
  [PlanetF1 — F1 Revisited](https://www.planetf1.com/features/f1-revisited-niki-lauda-1976-nurburgring-crash),
  [Wikipedia — 1976 German Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1976_German_Grand_Prix)
- **End of F1:** the **1976 German GP was the last F1 race on the Nordschleife** (the
  track had already been voted out for 1977 on safety grounds).
  Sources: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N%C3%BCrburgring),
  [PlanetF1](https://www.planetf1.com/features/f1-revisited-niki-lauda-1976-nurburgring-crash)
- **1982–83 rebuild:** more run-off (e.g. Aremberg, Brünnchen), a bypass shortening the
  lap to 20.832 km with a small pit lane, while the new GP-Strecke was built.
  Source: [RacingCircuits.info](https://www.racingcircuits.info/europe/germany/nurburgring.html)
- **Stefan Bellof, 28 May 1983:** qualifying for the Nürburgring 1000 km in a Rothmans
  **Porsche 956**, lapped in **6:11.13** (avg ~202 km/h) — the Nordschleife record for
  35 years.
  Sources: [Wikipedia — Stefan Bellof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefan_Bellof),
  [stefan-bellof.de](https://www.stefan-bellof.de/en-us/karriere/rekordrunde/)
- The **1983 1000 km** was the last major international race on the Nordschleife-only
  layout; the **GP-Strecke opened 12 May 1984** (Ayrton Senna won the opening
  Mercedes 190E celebrity race).
  Sources: [Wikipedia — 1983 1000 km of Nürburgring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1983_1000_km_of_N%C3%BCrburgring),
  [RacingCircuits.info](https://www.racingcircuits.info/europe/germany/nurburgring.html)

## 7. Caracciola-Karussell and Steilstrecke

- **Caracciola-Karussell:** banked 180°+ hairpin with a concrete-slab inner banking;
  banking commonly quoted at about **17 degrees**. Origin: in 1931 Rudolf Caracciola
  (idea credited to mechanic Wilhelm Sebastian) hooked the inside wheels into the
  drainage ditch for grip; the ditch was later paved into the banking.
  Sources: [Porsche Newsroom — Banking on the Nordschleife](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2020/motorsports/porsche-nuerburgring-nordschleife-caracciola-karussell-22455.html),
  [nring.info — Caracciola-Karussell](https://nring.info/nurburgring-nordschleife-corners/caracciola-karussell/),
  [oversteer48](https://oversteer48.com/nurburgring-carousel/).
  *FLAG: the 17° figure comes from enthusiast/secondary sources; no official banking
  spec found.*
- **Steilstrecke:** a ~**450 m** concrete test-hill section between Klostertal and Hohe
  Acht (cutting off the Karussell), gradient up to **27%**, opened **1928** for
  hill-start/test drives; long disused.
  Sources: [Circuits of the Past — Steilstrecke](https://www.circuitsofthepast.com/steilstrecke-nurburgring/),
  [Tarmac Timewarp](https://tarmac-timewarp.com/section-10-car-v-bike/)
